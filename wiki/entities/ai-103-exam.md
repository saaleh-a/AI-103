# Exam AI-103: Developing AI Apps and Agents on Azure

## Summary

AI-103 is the Microsoft certification exam for Azure AI engineers who build, manage, and deploy agents and AI solutions that use Microsoft Foundry (SRC-191 L102–105). The official study guide measured skills as of April 16, 2026 and groups the exam into five weighted domains (SRC-191 L100–118).

## What it is

The official audience is an Azure AI engineer who builds, manages, and deploys agents and AI solutions using Microsoft Foundry (SRC-191 L103–104). The candidate should have Python app-development experience and familiarity with general AI, generative AI, and Azure services (SRC-191 L104–105). Responsibilities include planning and managing Azure AI solutions, implementing generative AI and agentic solutions, computer vision solutions, text analysis solutions, and information extraction solutions (SRC-191 L106–112).

The Study Cram positions the transition plainly: “the AI 103 replaces the AI 102” and recommends using the Microsoft exam site and Learn modules rather than only watching the cram (SRC-1 “the AI 103 replaces the AI 102”; SRC-1 “look at the learn modules”).

## What the sources say

- The study guide notes that most questions cover GA features, but the exam may include Preview features if those features are commonly used (SRC-191 L100–101).
- The skills list is explicitly dated “Skills measured as of April 16, 2026” (SRC-191 L102).
- The course page links the course to the related Microsoft Certified: Azure AI Apps and Agents Developer Associate certification (SRC-27 L203–210).
- The course preview says completing AI-103 provides a foundation for understanding what to focus on for Microsoft Certified Azure AI Apps and Agents Developer Associate, AI-103, but also requires work experience and practice (SRC-28 L83–90).
- The Study Cram advises hands-on work: “don't just look at it” and “get the hands on” (SRC-1 “don't just look at it”; SRC-1 “get the hands on”).

## Capabilities and components

The official domain weights are: plan and manage an Azure AI solution at 25–30% (SRC-191 L114–119); implement generative AI and agentic solutions at 30–35% (SRC-191 L115–116; SRC-191 L140–159); implement computer vision solutions at 10–15% (SRC-191 L116; SRC-191 L160–179); implement text analysis solutions at 10–15% (SRC-191 L117; SRC-191 L180–190); and implement information extraction solutions at 10–15% (SRC-191 L118; SRC-191 L191–201).

For the detailed objective list, use [[objective-map]] rather than copying all 64 objectives here. The official study-guide scope includes planning, Foundry service/model choices, retrieval and indexing, deployment, CI/CD, quotas, monitoring, security, responsible AI, agent governance, generative applications, agents, workflows, evaluation, observability, multimodal vision, text/speech, and information extraction (SRC-191 L119–201).
