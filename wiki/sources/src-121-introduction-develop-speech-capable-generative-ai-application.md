## TL;DR

This introduction frames speech transcription and synthesis as useful capabilities for conversation documentation, captions, audible interfaces, and hands-free assistants. (SRC-121 L214–219) The module goal is to use speech-capable generative AI models in Microsoft Foundry to convert speech to text and text to speech. (SRC-121 L219)

## Key claims

- Speech transcription and synthesis are useful in calls and meetings, video captions, accessible audible interfaces, and hands-free AI assistants. (SRC-121 L214–218)
- The module explores speech-capable generative AI models in Microsoft Foundry for both speech-to-text and text-to-speech conversion. (SRC-121 L219)
- The Learn capture offers video and text-and-images formats, and says the text may contain greater detail than the video. (SRC-121 L212–221)

## How it works

The source is an orientation rather than a procedure. It establishes two directions for speech-capable generative AI: converting spoken audio into text and converting text into spoken audio. (SRC-121 L214–219)

## Key terms

- **Speech transcription:** the module introduces it as the speech-to-text side of the capability. (SRC-121 L214–219)
- **Speech synthesis:** the module introduces it as the text-to-speech side of the capability. (SRC-121 L214–219)
- **Microsoft Foundry:** the platform named for using speech-capable generative AI models in this module. (SRC-121 L219)

## Decision boundaries and exam cues

- **Inference:** If a scenario asks for captions, meeting documentation, or audio-to-text extraction, route toward transcription rather than synthesis. (SRC-121 L214–216, L219)
- **Inference:** If a scenario asks for audible interfaces or hands-free assistants that read messages aloud, route toward synthesis rather than transcription. (SRC-121 L217–219)
